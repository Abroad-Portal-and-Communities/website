Working in Germany as a foreign national. For EU-wide contract and document basics, see [Legal & documents (Europe)](/docs/working-abroad/eu/legal-and-documents/).

{{< callout type="warning" >}}
Rules change. Use official sources (e.g. [Make it in Germany](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/)) and consult immigration or legal advisers for your case.
{{< /callout >}}

## Common routes (overview)

| Route | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| EU Blue Card | Higher education + qualified job + salary threshold (adjusted yearly) |
| Skilled worker (Fachkraft) | Vocational or academic qualification recognized in Germany |
| ICT permit | Intra-company transfer within a group |
| Job seeker | Limited stay to search for qualified employment |
| EU free movement | For EU/EEA/Swiss citizens — registration, not a visa |

## Local steps (after entry)

Typical order; your city (`Ausländerbehörde`, `Bürgeramt`) may vary:

1. **Address registration** (*Anmeldung*) at the local registration office within the required deadline
2. **Residence title** — Collect or extend at the foreigners’ office if you entered with a visa vignette
3. **Tax ID** (*Steueridentifikationsnummer*) — Issued by tax office; needed for payroll
4. **Social insurance** — Usually arranged via employer (health, pension, unemployment)
5. **Bank account** — Many employers pay salary to a German IBAN
